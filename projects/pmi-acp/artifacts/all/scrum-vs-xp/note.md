# Scrum vs XP — Comparison

## One-line Summary
Scrum and XP are two agile frameworks that follow nearly identical processes but use different terminology — understanding the term mappings helps you switch between them and answer exam questions accurately.

## Objectives
- **What is it?** A side-by-side comparison of Scrum and XP terminology and roles.
- **What is it used for?** Clarifying that Scrum and XP are not opposites — they share the same iterative structure with different names.
- **When should you use it?** When choosing between frameworks or when an exam question uses XP-specific or Scrum-specific terms interchangeably.

## Key Points
- Every major Scrum ceremony, role, and artifact has a direct XP equivalent — just with a different name.
- XP uses programming-centric language; Scrum uses general project management language.
- **Scrum = general-purpose** (any product); **XP = software development only**.
- Both use fixed-length iterations (1–4 weeks, sometimes up to 6).
- The daily meeting is functionally identical in both: 15 minutes, same 3 questions.

## Mental Model
Think of Scrum and XP as two GPS apps giving the same directions in different voices. The route is the same — the words are different. Learning the term map lets you understand either "voice" instantly.

## Connections
| Scrum Term | XP Equivalent |
|---|---|
| Sprint | Iteration |
| Release | Small Release |
| Sprint Planning | Planning Game |
| Product Owner | Customer |
| Retrospective | Reflection |
| Scrum Master | XP Coach |
| Development Team | Programmers / Team |
| Daily Scrum | Daily Stand-up Meeting |

## Real-world Example
A company running Scrum decides to adopt XP practices for their dev team. Nothing about their weekly cadence changes — they still hold the same planning meeting at the start, the same daily check-in, and a reflection at the end. The only change is the vocabulary and the addition of XP engineering practices like TDD and pair programming.

## Pitfalls
- Thinking Scrum and XP are fundamentally different processes — they are **not**; the process is nearly identical.
- Mixing up **Retrospective** (Scrum) and **Reflection** (XP) — same meeting, different name.
- Confusing **Planning Game** (XP) with a casual activity — it is the formal equivalent of Sprint Planning.
- Assuming XP's **Customer** has a different authority level than the Product Owner — they have the same role: prioritize and represent the business.
- Forgetting that XP is **software-specific** while Scrum is not — this distinction matters for framework selection questions.

## Exam Tips
- Term-mapping questions are common: "What is the XP equivalent of a Scrum Master?" → **XP Coach**.
- "What is the XP equivalent of a Sprint?" → **Iteration** (same duration: 1–4 weeks).
- "What is a Planning Game?" → XP's version of **Sprint Planning / Release Planning**.
- Trap: questions may use XP and Scrum terms in the same scenario — recognize the mapping, don't be thrown off.
- If asked which framework is better for non-software work → **Scrum** (XP is software-only).
- **Retrospective = Reflection** — don't treat these as different activities on the exam.

## Self-check
- What is the XP equivalent of a Sprint? How long is it?
- What does XP call the Sprint Planning meeting?
- What is the XP equivalent of a Product Owner?
- What is the difference between a Retrospective (Scrum) and a Reflection (XP)?
- Which framework can be used outside of software development, and why?
- What are the three questions asked in both the Daily Scrum and the Daily Stand-up?
